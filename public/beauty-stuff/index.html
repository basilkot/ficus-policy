Beauty Stuff — Privacy Policy

Effective 6 September 2026

In short:

  • your collection lives on your device and works entirely offline, without signing in and without a subscription — just as before;
  • signing in and syncing are your choice. Turn them on and you create an account, and a copy of your collection together with its photos starts living in the cloud;
  • reading a product from a photo is a paid feature. The shot goes to our server and from there to Anthropic, which reads it; your collection as a whole is not sent;
  • crash reports and anonymous usage statistics still go out — Google services;
  • there are no ads, and your data is not sold to anyone.

Who is responsible for your data

Beauty Stuff is developed and maintained by an individual. Questions about data, deletion requests and anything else — write to kot.julic@gmail.com.

What is stored on your device

The contents of your collection live in your phone's memory:

  • products — name, brand, category, volume, price, comment, dates of purchase, opening and expiry;
  • product photos;
  • the ingredient list, if you typed it in or read it from a photo;
  • brand and category directories, care routines, shopping list;
  • app settings — for instance whether reminders are on and at what time.

Records live in the app's database, photos in the app's own directory. Neither is available to other apps. Uninstalling Beauty Stuff erases all of it along with the app.

Until you sign in, that is all there is: the app works offline, and nothing from your collection goes anywhere.

Your account

Signing in is needed for syncing and for the paid features; without them it is not required. Sign-in goes through your Google account, and accounts themselves are handled by Firebase Authentication, a service of Google LLC. What is kept about you:

  • the email address of your Google account — we use it to warn you before data is deleted;
  • an internal account identifier — your cloud data is found by it;
  • the name and the profile picture link from the same Google account — the app shows them at the top of the side menu so that you can see who is signed in. They go nowhere beyond your own screen.

The app never sees or stores your Google password: signing in is done by Android itself.

Syncing

You turn syncing on and you turn it off. While it is off, nothing listed below leaves your device.

While it is on, the contents of your collection are copied to the cloud:

  • products with all their fields, including comments and ingredient lists;
  • product photos — the full shots, not just the thumbnails;
  • brand and category directories, routines, shopping list;
  • records of what you deleted — so that a deletion reaches your second device.

Records are stored in Cloud Firestore, photos in Firebase Cloud Storage; both services belong to Google LLC. The data sits in a section marked with your identifier, and the access rules are written so that only someone signed in as you can read that section. We do not read the contents of your collection.

Turning syncing off stops the exchange but does not by itself erase what was already sent. How to erase it is below, under “How long this is kept”.

Reading photos and advice

These are paid features. They work through our server, which talks to a language model made by Anthropic PBC (USA). Nothing goes to it directly from the app.

What is sent

  • the shot you have just taken or picked;
  • the product photo from the card — as a second image, and only if it is already on your device. It helps make out what the product on the label is;
  • the fields you have already filled in — name, brand, category, volume;
  • the app language — so that the answer comes back in it;
  • for advice about your collection — a list of your products: category, brand, name, dates.

Your comments on products are never sent — neither when reading a photo nor for advice. They are the most personal thing in a collection, and they stay on the device.

What happens to it next

Anthropic receives the above in order to answer the request. How that company handles the data it receives is described in its privacy policy and developer terms.

What our own server keeps:

  • the model's answer — the extracted fields or ingredient list, together with a fingerprint of the request. It is what makes repeating the same request free: if the connection dropped or you cancelled the wait, the second attempt costs you nothing;
  • a monthly request counter — your remaining allowance is counted from it.

The photos themselves are not stored on our server. They pass through it to the model and are not kept.

Subscription

Payment goes through Google Play. Card details and payment information are handled by Google; the app never sees them. Google Play tells our server the state of your subscription — active, on hold or cancelled — and the server ties that to your account in order to unlock the paid features.

Verifying the app

So that our server is not called by other programs, the app uses Firebase App Check with Play Integrity: Google confirms that a request came from a genuine Beauty Stuff installed from Play. Nothing from your collection takes part in that check.

Crash reports and statistics

Crash reports — Firebase Crashlytics

When the app crashes or trips over an error, a technical report is sent:

  • the error type and the call stack;
  • device model, Android version, free memory;
  • app version and an internal installation identifier.

The contents of your collection, your photos and product names are not part of the report.

Usage statistics — Google Analytics for Firebase

The library collects anonymous information about usage:

  • app launches and moves between screens;
  • device model, Android version, language and approximate country;
  • the device advertising identifier (Google Advertising ID) — you can reset it or opt out in Android settings, under “Privacy → Ads”.

This shows how many people use the app and which screens they use. No user profile is built from it, and no ads are shown in the app.

On top of that, the app itself sends a few events about the purchase screen: whether it was opened, which feature brought you there, which tier and period were chosen, whether there was a free trial, and how it ended. This is what tells us whether the offer works, and what makes breakage visible — a purchase screen that opened empty, for instance.

Nothing from your collection goes into those events: what travels is the shape of the purchase, not the purchaser. No product names, no ingredients, no notes.

How Google handles the data it receives is described in Google's privacy policy and in the Firebase terms.

Backups

The backup you make

On “Save a backup” the app builds a zip archive with products, directories and photos and hands it to the system save dialog. Where the file goes is up to you: device storage, a memory card or cloud storage. The app does not send the archive anywhere by itself and has no access to your other files.

The backup Android makes

If Google backup is enabled in your device settings, the operating system copies the app's database and settings to your Google Drive by itself. Photos are not included in such a backup. This is done by Android, not by the app: Beauty Stuff has no access to the contents of that backup. It can be turned off in device settings, under “Google → Backup”.

How long this is kept

Data on your device lives until you delete it or uninstall the app.

The cloud copy lives as long as you use it. If there has been no syncing for six months, the copy is deleted along with the photos and the account. A month before that we write to the address of your Google account — so that you have time to come back and lose nothing, simply by opening the app. We never delete without warning.

Your collection on the phone stays where it is: only the cloud copy is deleted.

You can delete everything at once yourself: “Settings → Account → Delete account”. That erases the cloud records, the photos and the account itself.

Permissions and what they are for

  • Notifications — to remind you about expiring products. If you refuse, the app keeps working, but reminders will not arrive.
  • Camera — to read the “period after opening” symbol straight from the viewfinder. The permission is asked at the moment you open that reader and only for it; refusing closes only that. Nothing shot in this mode is saved anywhere.
  • Internet and network state — for signing in, syncing, the paid features, crash reports and statistics. The collection itself works offline.

Taking an ordinary product photo needs no permission: the shot is taken by the system camera app, which hands over the finished file. A photo from the gallery is picked through the system dialog, which passes on only the selected image. Beauty Stuff has no access to any other pictures on your device.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect their data.

Your rights

  • Data on your device is yours to control: any record can be changed or deleted in the app, and uninstalling the app erases everything at once.
  • The cloud copy and the account can be deleted in the app — “Settings → Account”. Syncing can be turned off without deleting anything.
  • To ask for the crash reports and statistics tied to your installation to be deleted, write to kot.julic@gmail.com. You will need the device advertising identifier — it is visible in Android settings.
  • Collection of the advertising identifier can be limited by Android itself, without uninstalling the app.

Changes to this policy

If what is collected changes, this text will be updated together with the app, and the date at the top will change with it. By continuing to use the app after an update you accept the new version.
